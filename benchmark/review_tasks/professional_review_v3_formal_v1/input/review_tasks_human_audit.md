# PFAS Professional Review Tasks: Human-Audit View

This view intentionally omits the defining-review identities and design
provenance. Reviewers should assess whether each focused task is realistic,
clear, non-leading, and suitable for professional literature synthesis.

## RT01. Selecting an adsorption medium for a defined PFAS water-treatment application

Prepare a focused, data-grounded review explaining how an engineer should
select an adsorption medium for a specified PFAS profile and water matrix.

1. Compare the adsorption mechanisms and material properties most relevant to
   PFAS chain length, functional group, and precursor composition.
2. Evaluate how dissolved organic matter, inorganic ions, pH, competing
   contaminants, and influent concentration alter performance.
3. Translate the evidence into conditional media-selection criteria rather
   than a universal material ranking.

## RT02. Translating PFAS adsorption results from batch tests to continuous treatment

Prepare a focused review of when batch adsorption results do and do not predict
column, pilot, or full-scale PFAS treatment performance.

1. Compare the information provided by equilibrium capacity, kinetics,
   fixed-bed breakthrough, bed volumes treated, and media lifetime.
2. Assess how flow, contact time, influent variability, competing constituents,
   and PFAS mixtures affect translation across scales.
3. Identify the minimum evidence needed to support a defensible service-life or
   scale-up claim.

## RT03. Deciding whether to regenerate or replace PFAS adsorption media

Prepare a focused review of the evidence needed to choose among regeneration,
replacement, and end-of-life management for PFAS-loaded adsorption media.

1. Compare regeneration approaches by PFAS release or destruction, recovered
   capacity, repeated-cycle performance, and material deterioration.
2. Account for liquid, gaseous, or solid residuals and distinguish transfer of
   PFAS from actual destruction.
3. Evaluate the operational, energy, chemical, cost, and sustainability
   evidence needed for a site-specific decision.

## RT04. Selecting a membrane process for a defined PFAS feed water

Prepare a focused, comparative review of how membrane processes should be
selected for a specified PFAS composition, water matrix, and treatment
objective.

1. Compare nanofiltration, reverse osmosis, adsorptive membranes, electrically
   assisted membranes, and relevant hybrid configurations by separation
   mechanism.
2. Evaluate effects of PFAS properties, membrane characteristics, pressure or
   flux, recovery, ions, organic matter, and fouling.
3. Define when reported rejection, adsorption, permeability, and energy results
   are sufficiently comparable to support process selection.

## RT05. Managing PFAS residuals from membrane treatment

Prepare a focused review of how membrane-derived PFAS concentrates and spent
membrane materials should be characterized and managed within a treatment
train.

1. Determine how recovery, concentration factor, PFAS composition, and
   co-contaminants define the retained stream.
2. Compare downstream concentration, destruction, recycle, disposal, and
   hybrid-treatment options using compatible endpoints.
3. Assess how residual management changes the energy, cost, environmental
   burden, and practical value of membrane separation.

## RT06. Identifying the operating conditions that control PFAS foam fractionation

Prepare a focused review of how PFAS properties, water chemistry, and reactor
operation control foam-fractionation performance.

1. Evaluate effects of PFAS chain length and functional group, co-surfactants,
   organic matter, salts, and competing constituents.
2. Compare gas flow, bubble size, residence time, recycle, foam collection, and
   reactor configuration.
3. Interpret removal, recovery, enrichment, mass balance, and volume reduction
   as distinct performance measures.

## RT07. Placing foam fractionation within a PFAS treatment train

Prepare a focused review of when foam fractionation is useful as a
concentration step and how the resulting PFAS-rich foamate should be managed.

1. Compare evidence from laboratory, pilot, and commercial applications for
   different feed waters and PFAS profiles.
2. Assess short-chain PFAS recovery, achievable volume reduction, stability of
   operation, and energy demand.
3. Evaluate downstream treatment or disposal of foamate and identify conditions
   under which the overall treatment train is advantageous.

## RT08. Matching a destructive technology to a PFAS-rich liquid stream

Prepare a focused review of how electrochemical oxidation, non-thermal plasma,
and sonolysis should be matched to a defined PFAS-rich liquid stream.

1. Compare the reaction environments, feed requirements, and PFAS structures
   for which each technology has been evaluated.
2. Assess effects of concentration, salinity, co-contaminants, reactor
   operation, kinetics, and energy demand.
3. Translate laboratory, pilot, and field evidence into conditional
   technology-selection criteria.

## RT09. Determining whether a PFAS destructive treatment achieved meaningful destruction

Prepare a focused review of the measurements needed to distinguish
disappearance of a target PFAS from transformation, defluorination, and broader
destruction.

1. Compare parent-compound loss, transformation-product analysis, fluoride
   release, fluorine mass balance, and mineralization-related endpoints.
2. Evaluate how analytical scope, sampling time, detection limits, and
   unidentified products affect interpretation.
3. Define a defensible evidence hierarchy for reporting destruction while
   preserving technology, matrix, and scale limitations.

## RT10. Designing PFAS treatment studies that remain valid in real water

Prepare a focused review of how water-matrix effects should be tested and
reported so that PFAS treatment performance can be translated beyond clean
laboratory water.

1. Compare how organic matter, surfactants, inorganic ions, and other co-solutes
   alter physical separation and degradation processes.
2. Distinguish inhibitory, promotive, neutral, and interactive effects and
   relate them to treatment mechanism and PFAS properties.
3. Recommend a minimum matrix-characterization and matched-control framework
   for defensible cross-study comparison.
